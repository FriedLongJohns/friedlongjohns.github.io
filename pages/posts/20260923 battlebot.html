PLANT NHRL Battlebot

I started working on an antweight battlebots robot

Ever watched Battlebots on TV? I think it's pretty fun watching 250-lb robots tear each other apart. So when I heard they've got a similar thing at Purdue...

Awww yeah.

The club I'm in is competes in NHRL. A much lighter game than battlebots, competitions have 3, 12, and 30 lb brackets. For our beginner project, my team is fighting at our school's local Plastic Antweight (PLANT) competition.

Primary design iterations

Our team wanted to build a Melty bot - a type of bot that spins it's entire body as a weapon in order to get much more energy out of it. The more senior members convinced me we should do a ring spinner archetype, so I got to work on a brainstorming version.

Pretty rough, but you can already see the frontal brushless motor driving a belted pulley in order to save vertical space.

1 / 3

Ring spinners are pretty heavy, so we decided to compact the design by fitting the motors much closer together. We considered gears and chains, but both would deal poorly with massive impacts during matches.

I also discovered that the entire top of the brushless motor rotates, so I changed the design to let the motor directly drive the weapon ring with friction, using O-rings mounted on a 3d printed cover.

Do mind the decorations on the direct drive's baseplate.


While our ring was retained with one driving and two bearing wheels along the equator, this leaves the ring vulnerable to up/down attacks like saws or flippers. Most ring bots retain their rings by having vertical bearings as well as horizontal ones, but bearings are heavy and we want to save weight.

So I took some inspiration from a much bigger battlebots brother: The Ringmaster.

First Test Prints

With the ring problem (somewhat) solved, we made some test prints.

You can see the battery access panel splits this top plate design into 2 pieces. Not a good idea, in terms of strength.

The second test print has much of the walls and lower motor blocks directly printed into the bottom plate, and I've even put in the power switch to check it's fit. Most of the screw holes worked first try, fortunately.

1 / 3

That's about as far as this project has gotten. This post will be updated as the project progresses!
